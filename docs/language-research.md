# Guide language selection

Research checked: 2026-10-06. Recommendation: **eight languages in total**, including the existing English guide.

| Guide locale | Reader-facing name | Steam language share, September 2026 | Why include it |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| `en` | English | 34.20% | Existing source language and broad international access. |
| `es-MX` | Español | 4.83% combined Spanish entries | Mexican Spanish with neutral Latin American phrasing; also useful to readers elsewhere. |
| `pt-BR` | Português (Brasil) | 3.70% Brazilian Portuguese | Substantial gaming audience; Mega Man 11 provides a Brazilian Portuguese localization. |
| `ja` | 日本語 | 2.41% | The series' original language; included in the examined releases. |
| `zh-Hans` | 简体中文 | 31.39% | Largest non-English Steam language; Battle Network supports this script. |
| `fr` | Français | 2.08% | Large international language; official collection interfaces support it. |
| `de` | Deutsch | 2.48% | Established gaming audience and official collection interfaces. |
| `ru` | Русский | 9.05% | Strongest remaining Steam reach within this eight-language budget. |

Shares are from [Valve's September 2026 survey](https://store.steampowered.com/hwsurvey/), an optional anonymous sample of Steam customers' selected languages. Spanish combines Spain (4.08%) and Latin America (0.75%). The chosen entries total **90.14% of that sample**, calculated from those rounded values. This is not 90.14% of all gamers, unique speakers or Mega Man fans. Monthly samples fluctuate; Steam also excludes console-only players.

## Speaker reach

Population is a useful second check, rather than the sole ranking criterion. The [USITC staff's March 2024 DICL paper, Table 1](https://www.usitc.gov/sites/default/files/publications/332/working_papers/ghty_2024_dicl_language_database.pdf) reports total first- and second-language users from **Ethnologue's 2018 edition**: English 1,122 million, Mandarin Chinese 1,107 million, Spanish 513 million, French 285 million, Russian 264 million and Portuguese 237 million. These are historical scale indicators, **not current 2026 counts**. Mandarin population is not a count of Simplified Chinese readers; Portuguese population is not exclusively Brazilian.

Two further indicators establish the scale of the other choices: the [German government's language page](https://www.make-it-in-germany.com/de/leben-in-deutschland/deutsch-lernen/deutschkurse), checked on the research date, gives approximately **130 million** first- or second-language German speakers. The [British Council's 2013 report, page 7](https://www.britishcouncil.org/sites/default/files/languages-for-the-future-report-v3.pdf) gives **122 million native Japanese speakers**. The latter is older and excludes second-language speakers; it cannot be used as a directly comparable current total.

For more recent Spanish context, [Instituto Cervantes' 2025 summary](https://cvc.cervantes.es/lengua/anuario/anuario_25/elm/p01.htm) reports over 630 million potential users and around 520 million native-proficiency speakers. Its broader definition includes people with limited proficiency and learners, so it should not replace a comparable fluent-speaker figure in a cross-language ranking.

Direct current Ethnologue pages could not be inspected because access returned HTTP 403. Rather than copy unsourced current numbers from language-marketing websites, retain the dates and definitions above.

## Evidence specific to Mega Man

No reliable public census of **Mega Man fans by language** was found. Official localization choices indicate that Capcom serves particular languages; they do not measure the number of fans in those languages.

The publisher-maintained Steam entries inspected on 2026-10-06 provide these boundaries:

- [Mega Man 11](https://store.steampowered.com/app/742300/Mega_Man_11/) is a standalone game. It lists interface and subtitle support for English, French, German, Japanese, both Chinese scripts, Latin American Spanish and Brazilian Portuguese, among other languages. This is stronger game-level evidence than a collection's interface list, but does not verify any particular boss, weapon or stage translation.
- [Battle Network Legacy Collection Vol. 1](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1798010/Mega_Man_Battle_Network_Legacy_Collection_Vol_1/) lists English, Japanese and both Chinese scripts with interface/subtitle support. The guide can provide instructions in additional languages without claiming the games themselves include those translations.
- [X Legacy Collection 2](https://store.steampowered.com/app/743900/Mega_Man_X_Legacy_Collection_2/) lists English, French, German, Spanish, Japanese and Traditional Chinese interfaces, among others. Traditional Chinese has no subtitle check in its table. These launcher-level declarations do not establish translated text for every bundled title.
- [Zero/ZX Legacy Collection](https://store.steampowered.com/app/999020/Mega_Man_ZeroZX_Legacy_Collection/) lists French, German, Spanish and Japanese interface/subtitle support and both Chinese interfaces without Chinese subtitle checks. It bundles different games with different histories. Do not infer that all six games share every listed translation.
- A [Capcom Customer Service developer reply, 2017-08-08](https://steamcommunity.com/app/495050/discussions/0/1471967615861119635/) explicitly confirms that Classic Mega Man 7–10 can run their original Japanese Rockman versions after selecting Japanese in the collection options. This establishes Japanese versions for those titles; it is not a glossary of their localized stage names.

## Russian, Korean and other candidates

Russian is recommended ahead of Korean for the eighth slot: its observed Steam share is substantially larger than Korean's **1.37%**. Neither language appears in the inspected Mega Man collection support tables. That makes this an audience-reach decision, not a claim that Russia has more Mega Man fans or better localized game coverage.

Traditional Chinese (**1.27%** in the same Steam survey) is a strong next candidate because multiple collections explicitly support that interface and Battle Network supports subtitles. It is a distinct written locale; a Simplified Chinese translation should not be relabeled as Traditional without a separate terminology and script review. Korean, Italian and Polish are sensible subsequent candidates. Hindi and Arabic have large speaker populations but population alone does not establish this guide's likely gaming audience.

## Translation and naming rules

1. **Translate the instructions and interface; retain canonical English game identifiers until an official alias is verified.** This applies to stage selectors, boss names, weapons, items, armor, chips, forms, mission names and map labels. It also keeps the guide easy to compare with the unchanged English screenshots. Descriptive pickup headings and Battle Network chapter headings written for this guide are ordinary guide prose, not official game identifiers; their descriptions can be translated while actual names inside them stay English.
2. A collection launcher, web manual, storefront locale or fan translation is not enough to prove an individual game's official stage wording. Require an actual game screen, official manual text naming that identifier, or another publisher-authored title-specific source. Record game, platform/version, language, English identifier, official alias and evidence before enabling an alias.
3. Original Japanese names are not necessarily literal translations of the English release. In particular, do not manufacture Japanese boss aliases by translating English animal names. Use verified title-specific names, or keep the English name.
4. Screenshots remain English, as requested. Keep existing source credits, assets and enlargement behavior. Translate descriptive alternative text without altering the pictured text or implying the screenshot is localized.
5. Spanish follows the user-confirmed `es-MX` preference: tú for the individual player, ustedes for plural; control and computadora; never vosotros, coger, mando or ordenador. Use direct, neutral instructional phrasing. Portuguese uses `pt-BR` consistently.
6. Keep numbers, route order, requirements, warnings and timing unchanged. Missable-event instructions need semantic review: a translated sentence must preserve what triggers the event and when an opportunity is lost.
7. Publish only language/guide combinations with actual translated body text. Label any fallback as English; a translated navigation bar alone is not a translated guide. Native-speaker gameplay review remains distinct from structural completeness.

Language choices are a recommendation based on the evidence available on this date. Revisit the next slots once there is genuine reader feedback or a larger translation budget; no tracking system is required just to justify these eight.

## Common interface copy

`src/locales/ui.json` contains manually authored common interface messages for these eight locales. This file is separate from the agent-authored guide prose. It covers completion controls, stage/mission/chapter counters, storage failures, sidebar controls, image closing, language selection, compact combat labels and translation disclosures. The visible completion label stays fixed in either state; the check, color and accessible pressed state communicate the user's selection.

All locales have the same 26 keys and matching named placeholders. Structural checks confirm 208 non-empty messages and reject the prohibited Peninsular Spanish terms. These checks establish completeness and substitution safety, not native-speaker editorial sign-off. The translation credit says that the work is AI-assisted with selected text reviewed. It explicitly states that a full native-speaker review has not been completed; it does not claim that every gameplay term or paragraph has been reviewed.
